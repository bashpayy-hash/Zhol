Three people, one evening

The owner buys, the dispatcher uses it, the forwarder needs the same answer for loads they subcontract. Each one has a different evening.

The buyer

The owner · il titolare

Their evening: calls from customers asking where the goods are, and drivers asking whether to leave.

Who: on average 55 years old, running a firm with margins under 3% of revenue.

What hurts: diesel at €2.13 a litre in April 2026, about €9,000 more per truck a year; and the legal €100 an hour for waiting is almost impossible for a small firm to collect.

Pays today: telematics €10–35 per truck a month, a freight-exchange seat €120–200, a TMS €50–100.

We ask: what did the last disruption cost you, and who signs the cheque?

The user

The dispatcher · l’ufficio traffico

Their evening: tomorrow’s plan in Excel or the TMS, then six country calendars, the Tyrol dosing PDF, two WhatsApp groups and the news, checked by hand.

What hurts: finding out at 05:00 from a driver in the queue at Kufstein what could have been known at 18:00.

Earns: about €28–36k gross a year in job ads; German and TIMOCOM for international desks.

What we must check: not one job ad mentions evening work or WhatsApp. Whether tomorrow’s plan is really made the evening before is the first thing our interviews test.

We ask: when is tomorrow’s plan final, and how many minutes do you spend checking roads?

The second buyer

The forwarder · lo spedizioniere

Their evening: loads placed with several hauliers, a customer who wants one answer, no visibility on the subcontractor’s route.

What hurts: the customer blames the forwarder for a closed pass the haulier knew nothing about either.

Pays today: visibility tools are paid by large shippers, not by a 20-person forwarder.

We ask: how often do you re-plan a load because of a road restriction abroad?

Four segments, one order

A

Alpine lanes from the North · ≈2,350 offices

Hauliers with 6–100 trucks in Lombardy, Veneto, Emilia-Romagna, Piemonte and Trentino-Alto Adige, weekly to DE, AT, CH, FR. Brenner dosing, Luegbrücke, night bans, French and German holidays. We start here.

B

South–North long-haul · ≈2,000 firms

Sicily, Campania and Puglia to the North and abroad, much of it fresh produce. Spontaneous blockades, the Messina strait, ferries. October 2026: about 100 trucks a day stopped at Vittoria alone.

C

Austrian, German and Slovenian hauliers on Italian lanes · ≈2,000–4,000, rough

The same crossings from the other side: Italian Sunday bans, strikes, the A22. Same engine, new language.

D

Forwarders · ≈800 with a road desk, ≈450 in the North

About 1,600 member firms in Fedespedi, more than 700 in ALSEA (Milano, Lodi, Pavia). They buy per shipment for loads they place with hauliers.

Il Post, 21 Oct 2026 (Vittoria) · Fedespedi · ALSEA · segment sizes are estimates, method in docs/09-audience.md

Dosing stays

The EU Advocate General found three Tyrolean bans unlawful but dosing compatible with EU law (16 July 2026). If the Court agrees, dosing becomes Tyrol’s main tool.

Not in any calendar

The October blockades were spontaneous, not called by the national associations: 288 protest points were mapped in the South.

Scarce drivers

About 20,000 freight drivers are missing in Italy (ANITA). Every driver-hour in a queue is capacity a haulier does not have.

CURIA press release, 16 Jul 2026 · Conftrasporto via trans.info · Geopop · ANITA via QN, 1 Jul 2026 · full list with sources in docs/08-people-and-voices.md
